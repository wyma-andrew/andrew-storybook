The Last Lighthouse

INT. ASHFALL LIGHTHOUSE — LANTERN ROOM — NIGHT

Rain hammers the glass. MIRA KADE, 34, wipes salt off the 1890s optic with a rag that has seen better decades. The lamp stutters. Comes back. Stutters again.

Mira
Not tonight. Please. Not tonight.

She thumbs the breaker. The tower groans. Below her, somewhere in the dark water, a horn sounds twice — wrong rhythm, wrong ship.

CUT TO:
EXT. BLACK ROCK COVE — CONTINUOUS

Slate beach. Fog thick as wet wool. The wrecked trawler hull lies half-buried, ribs to the sky. A figure walks out of the surf — THE DRIFTER, late 20s, soaked, barefoot, carrying nothing.

Old Tobin (O.S.)
Don't. Whatever it is, girl — don't bring it inside.
CUT TO:
INT. THE TIDE CELLAR — LATER

Ankle-deep seawater. OLD TOBIN, 71, holds a storm lantern in his four-fingered hand. The Drifter sits on the stone step, dripping, silent. Mira crouches, level with them.
